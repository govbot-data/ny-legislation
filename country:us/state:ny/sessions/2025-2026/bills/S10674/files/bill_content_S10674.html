Add Art 22-A §§265 - 269, amd §§251-z-2 & 500-a, Ag & Mkts L
 
Establishes the home kitchen enterprise and economic opportunity act; relates to the licensing and operation of home kitchen operations which store, handle, prepare and package ready-to-eat food for consumers.

        AN  ACT  to  amend  the  agriculture and markets law, in relation to the
          licensing of home kitchen operations
 
          The People of the State of New York, represented in Senate and  Assem-
        bly, do enact as follows:

     1    Section  1.   Short title. This act shall be known and may be cited as
     2  the "home kitchen enterprise and economic opportunity act".
     3    § 2.   Legislative intent. Every day,  New  Yorkers  are  using  their
     4  skills, talents and creativity to build home kitchen-based businesses to
     5  provide  unique, quality food products, increasingly sourced using local
     6  ingredients for direct sale to  consumers,  farmers  markets  and  other
     7  retail  outlets.  By  practicing  and perfecting their products, many of
     8  these craft culinary enterprises have succeeded and grown into  thriving
     9  and  vibrant small businesses, creating jobs and supporting local econo-
    10  mies and local farmers. It is the legislature's intent that home  kitch-
    11  en-based  food entrepreneurs should be supported and encouraged in their
    12  efforts. Homemade food businesses can create significant economic oppor-
    13  tunities for New Yorkers that need  them  most--including  women,  immi-
    14  grants,  and  people  of  color, who have historically faced barriers to
    15  labor force participation and entrepreneurship and  many  of  whom  have
    16  lost employment in the food industry as a result of the COVID-19 pandem-
    17  ic.  The home kitchen enterprise and economic opportunity act modernizes
    18  regulations impacting these enterprises and reduces artificial  barriers
    19  to business growth, while preserving food safety and public health.
    20    § 3.  The agriculture and markets law is amended by adding a new arti-
    21  cle 22-A to read as follows:
    22                                ARTICLE 22-A
    23                           HOME KITCHEN OPERATIONS
    24  Section 265. Definitions.
    25          266. Licenses; fees.
    26          267. Sales, delivery, disclosure.
    27          268. Rules and regulations.
    28          269. Inspections.
 
         EXPLANATION--Matter in italics (underscored) is new; matter in brackets

     1    §  265.  Definitions.  1. "Home kitchen operation" means an enterprise
     2  in a private home that is controlled and operated  by  a  resident  that
     3  stores, handles, prepares and packages food for consumers and that meets
     4  all of the following conditions:
     5    (a)  all  food  sold  or  otherwise  provided to consumers by the home
     6  kitchen operation is ready-to-eat food; and
     7    (b) does not produce:   alcoholic beverages; milk  or  milk  products;
     8  food  items  containing  raw  shellfish; any food that requires a hazard
     9  analysis and critical control point plan under applicable federal, state
    10  or local law; and any  low-acid  canned  food  or  acidified  food  that
    11  requires  a  scheduled  process under applicable federal, state or local
    12  law.
    13    A home kitchen operation does not include  a  processor  of  home-pro-
    14  cessed  food  operating  under  an  exemption from licensing pursuant to
    15  article twenty-C of this chapter authorized by the commissioner, a  food
    16  service establishment, temporary food service establishment, caterer, or
    17  residential group home facility.
    18    2.  "Licensed  area" means the kitchen of a private home and any other
    19  area of the home used for the preparation, packaging, storage,  handling
    20  or  sanitation of food, equipment, and utensils used in the home kitchen
    21  operation, including refuse and toilet areas.
    22    3.  "Ready-to-eat food" means food that is in a form  that  is  edible
    23  and  safe  to  eat without washing, cooking or additional preparation by
    24  the consumer and that is reasonably expected to be consumed in the  form
    25  in  which  it  is  provided  to  the  consumer or after reheating by the
    26  consumer.
    27    § 266. Licenses; fees. 1.  No person shall maintain or operate a  home
    28  kitchen operation unless licensed by the commissioner. Application for a
    29  license  to  operate  a home kitchen operation shall be made upon a form
    30  prescribed by the commissioner. The commissioner may require a  licensee
    31  to renew the license annually.
    32    2.  A  license, once issued, shall be nontransferable. A license shall
    33  be valid only for the person and location specified by such license and,
    34  unless suspended or revoked for cause, for the time period indicated.
    35    3. The license, or an accurate copy thereof, shall be retained by  the
    36  operator  onsite  and made available upon request to a representative of
    37  the department.
    38    4.  The applicant for a home kitchen operation license shall submit to
    39  the commissioner written standard operating procedures that include  all
    40  of the following information:
    41    (a) a list of all food types or products that will be handled;
    42    (b) proposed procedures and methods of food preparation and handling;
    43    (c)  procedures,  methods,  and  schedules  for  cleaning utensils and
    44  equipment and for the disposal of refuse;
    45    (d) how food will be maintained at safe holding  temperatures  pending
    46  pickup by consumers or during delivery;
    47    (e) days and times that the home kitchen may be used as a home kitchen
    48  operation.  The  stated days and times shall be for information purposes
    49  and not binding on the licensee.
    50    5. The commissioner shall issue a license after an initial  inspection
    51  has  determined  that the proposed home kitchen operation and its method
    52  of operation comply with the requirements of this article.  The  initial
    53  inspection may be conducted remotely via videoconference technology.
    54    6.  The  commissioner  shall  provide  the operator the opportunity to
    55  update the food types and products handled without requiring the  opera-
    56  tor to submit a new license application.

        S. 10674                            3
 
     1    7. The commissioner may charge a license fee not to exceed one hundred
     2  seventy-five dollars.
     3    8. The commissioner may decline to grant a new license, may decline to
     4  renew  a  license,  and  may suspend or revoke a license already granted
     5  after due notice and opportunity for hearing whenever he  or  she  finds
     6  that:
     7    (a)  any statement contained in an application for a license is or was
     8  false or misleading;
     9    (b) the home kitchen operation does not have facilities  or  equipment
    10  sufficient to maintain adequate sanitation for the activities conducted;
    11    (c)  the home kitchen operation is not maintained in a clean and sani-
    12  tary condition or is not operated in a sanitary manner;
    13    (d) the maintenance and operation of the  home  kitchen  operation  is
    14  such that the food produced therein is or may be adulterated;
    15    (e)  the  home  kitchen operation has failed or refused to produce any
    16  records or provide any information demanded by the commissioner  reason-
    17  ably related to the administration and enforcement of this article; or
    18    (f)  the  home  kitchen operation has failed to comply with any of the
    19  provisions of this article or rules and regulations promulgated pursuant
    20  thereto.
    21    § 267. Sales, delivery, disclosure. 1.   Home kitchen  operations  may
    22  sell or otherwise provide food directly to consumers only and not to any
    23  food  reseller.    Home kitchen operations may not sell or deliver their
    24  food to consumers outside New York.
    25    2. Home kitchen operations may sell food to  consumers  in  person  or
    26  remotely  by  telephone,  email or internet website, including through a
    27  third-party's internet website or mobile application. Food from  a  home
    28  kitchen  operation  may be delivered to consumers by the operator, by an
    29  employee or agent of the operator, or by a third-party delivery service.
    30    3. Home kitchen operations shall provide the following information  to
    31  the  consumer  in  a  clear and conspicuous manner on a label affixed to
    32  food that is sold in a package or container, on a sign at the  point  of
    33  sale  if  the  food  is  not  sold in a package or container, and on any
    34  webpage or mobile application where the food can be ordered:
    35    (a) the name, license number, and telephone number of the home kitchen
    36  operation that prepared the food;
    37    (b) the common or usual name of the food item;
    38    (c) if a food item contains more than one ingredient, a  list  of  its
    39  ingredients in descending order of predominance by weight; and
    40    (d) the following statement: "Made in a home kitchen."
    41    §  268.  Rules  and  regulations. 1. By no later than December thirty-
    42  first, two thousand  twenty-seven,  the  commissioner  shall  promulgate
    43  rules  and  regulations to implement this article.  Such rules and regu-
    44  lations may include provisions relating to  licensing  of  home  kitchen
    45  operations,  hygiene, sanitation, approved sources of food, training and
    46  recordkeeping.
    47    2. The regulations promulgated pursuant to  subdivision  one  of  this
    48  section  shall include requirements for sanitation in home kitchen oper-
    49  ations that shall:
    50    (a) apply only to the licensed operations and  licensed  area  of  the
    51  home;
    52    (b)  be  reasonably  necessary  to  ensure  food safety and reasonably
    53  feasible for ordinary home kitchen facilities;
    54    (c) not require home kitchen operations to have or to  use  facilities
    55  or equipment not ordinarily used in private homes; and

        S. 10674                            4
 
     1    (d)  not  prevent  or  restrict persons who live in the home and their
     2  guests from accessing and being present in the licensed area while  food
     3  for  the  home  kitchen  operation is being stored, handled, prepared or
     4  packaged, so long as such persons are supervised by the licensed  opera-
     5  tor  and not known to have symptoms of acute gastrointestinal illness or
     6  to be infected with a disease that is transmissible through food.
     7    3. If food safety training is required, the commissioner shall  ensure
     8  that  the training content and any required examination are available in
     9  languages other than English that are commonly spoken by New York  resi-
    10  dents as their primary language.
    11    4.  Regulations  adopted  by  the  commissioner  may require that home
    12  kitchen operations comply with retail food store sanitation regulations,
    13  provided that home kitchen operations shall be exempt from any  require-
    14  ments that do not meet the criteria of subdivision two of this section.
    15    5.  A  home  kitchen  operation  shall comply with the rules and regu-
    16  lations adopted by the commissioner under this article.
    17    § 269. Inspections. 1.  After the initial inspection for  purposes  of
    18  determining  license  eligibility,  a  home  kitchen  operation shall be
    19  subject to inspections only in the following three circumstances:
    20    (a) Not more than once in any twelve-month period, the department  may
    21  conduct  a  routine inspection for the purpose of observing the operator
    22  engaged in the usual activities of a home kitchen operation,  including,
    23  but  not  limited  to,  active  food  preparation.  The department shall
    24  provide notice to an operator before  a  routine  inspection  and  shall
    25  conduct  the  routine  inspection at a mutually agreeable date and time.
    26  This paragraph shall not be deemed to require the department to  conduct
    27  a routine inspection.
    28    (b)  A for-cause inspection may be conducted when the department has a
    29  valid reason, such as a credible consumer  complaint,  to  suspect  that
    30  adulterated  or  otherwise  unsafe  food  has  been produced by the home
    31  kitchen operation or that the home kitchen operation has otherwise  been
    32  in  violation of this article. The department shall provide notice to an
    33  operator before conducting a for-cause inspection and shall conduct  the
    34  inspection at a mutually agreeable date and time.
    35    (c)  If the department has just cause to believe that the home kitchen
    36  operation poses a serious hazard or immediate threat to  public  health,
    37  the  department  may  conduct an emergency inspection that is limited to
    38  the facts prompting the inspection. The  department  shall  provide  the
    39  operator  with  advance  notice of an emergency inspection to the extent
    40  that it is reasonable to do so under the circumstances.
    41    2. The department may inspect only  the  licensed  area  of  the  home
    42  kitchen  operation  for  the  purpose of enforcing or administering this
    43  article.
    44    § 4. Subdivision 3 of section 251-z-2 of the agriculture  and  markets
    45  law,  as  amended by chapter 507 of the laws of 1973, is amended to read
    46  as follows:
    47    3. The term "food processing  establishment"  means  any  place  which
    48  receives  food  or food products for the purpose of processing or other-
    49  wise adding to  the  value  of  the  product  for  commercial  sale.  It
    50  includes,  but  is not limited to, bakeries, processing plants, beverage
    51  plants and food manufactories. However, the term does not include: those
    52  establishments that process and manufacture food or food  products  that
    53  are  sold  exclusively  at  retail for consumption on the premises; home
    54  kitchen operations  licensed  by  the  commissioner  under  section  two
    55  hundred  sixty-six  of this chapter; those operations which cut meat and
    56  sell such meat at retail on the premises; bottled and bulk water facili-

     1  ties; those food processing establishments which are covered by articles
     2  four, four-a, five-a, five-b,  five-c,  five-d,  seventeen-b,  nineteen,
     3  [twenty-b,] and twenty-one of this chapter; service food establishments,

     9  or  distribution  of  one or more raw agricultural commodities which are

    12    §  5.  Subdivisions  1  and  2 of section 500-a of the agriculture and
    13  markets law, as amended by chapter 459 of the laws of 2025, are  amended
    14  to read as follows:
    15    1. "Food service establishment" means any place where food is prepared
    16  and  intended  for  individual portion service, and includes the site at
    17  which individual portions are provided, whether consumption occurs on or
    18  off the premises, or whether or not there is a charge for the food.  The
    19  term "food service establishment" does not include a home kitchen opera-
    20  tion licensed by the commissioner under section two hundred sixty-six of
    21  this chapter.
    22    2. "Retail food store" means any establishment or section of an estab-
    23  lishment where food and food products are offered to  the  consumer  and
    24  intended for off-premises consumption. The term "retail food store" does
    25  not  include  home kitchen operations licensed by the commissioner under
    26  section two hundred sixty-six  of  this  chapter,  establishments  which
    27  handle  only  pre-packaged,  non-potentially  hazardous  foods, roadside
    28  markets that offer only fresh fruits and fresh vegetables for sale, food
    29  service establishments, or food and beverage vending machines.
    30    § 6.  This act shall take effect immediately.
