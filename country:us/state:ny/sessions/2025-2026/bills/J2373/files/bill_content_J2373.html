J02373 Text:

 
Senate Resolution No. 2373
 
BY: Senator WEBB
 
        MOURNING  the death of Gloria Steinem, journalist,
        social activist, and Feminist icon
 
  WHEREAS, It is the custom of this Legislative Body to pay tribute to
citizens of the State of New York whose  lifework  and  civic  endeavors
served to enhance the quality of life in their communities and the great
State of New York; and
 
  WHEREAS, Gloria Steinem died on Wednesday, September 2, 2026, in her
home in New York City, New York at the age of 92; and
 
  WHEREAS,  Born  in Toledo, Ohio, on March 25, 1934, Gloria Steinem's
mother, Ruth,  suffered  from  mental  health  issues  and  her  parents
eventually split up when she was 10, leaving her largely to care for her
mother for the next seven years; and
 
  WHEREAS, After she finished high school, Gloria Steinem moved to the
Washington  D.C.  area  to  live with her older sister and then attended
Smith College in Massachusetts where, after  graduation,  she  became  a
freelance writer, profiling people from Paul Newman to Pat Nixon; and
 
  WHEREAS,  Gloria  Steinem's  zest for social journalism and activism
led her to delve into the inequities of American society when, in  1963,
she  went  undercover  in  New  York's  Playboy  Club for her subsequent
article "I Was a Playboy Bunny" which garnered national attention to the
indignities of those working as  hostesses  while  offering  sympathetic
portraits of the women; and
 
  WHEREAS, Gloria Steinem was a supporter of the 1968 presidential bid
of  South  Dakota  Sen. George McGovern, an early foe of the Vietnam War
and whom Steinem found to be an "unpretentious, honest man"; and
 
  WHEREAS, In 1971, Gloria Steinem  co-founded  the  National  Women's
Political  Caucus, a group that brought together a powerhouse collection
of leaders including New York Representatives Bella  Abzug  and  Shirley
Chisholm,   "Feminine   Mystique"  author  Betty  Friedan  and  longtime
activists Fannie Lou Hamer, Myrlie Evers,  LaDonna  Harris  and  Dorothy
Height; and
 
  WHEREAS,  Also in 1971, Gloria Steinem co-founded Ms. Magazine which
became wildly popular and  gave  a  national  voice  to  Women's  Rights
movements and Feminism as a whole; and
 
  WHEREAS,  The  magazine  made  history  by  publishing  a list of 53
prominent women who had undergone abortions,  "Our  purpose  is  not  to
alienate  or  to  ask  for  sympathy, but to repeal archaic and inhumane
laws,"  the  accompanying  text  stated;  months  later,  abortion   was
legalized nationally in the Supreme Court's Roe v. Wade ruling; and
 
  WHEREAS,   Throughout  the  decades  of  her  work,  Gloria  Steinem
championed  social  causes  like  the  Equal  Rights   Amendment,   even
testifying to the Senate in 1970, in support of ratifying the amendment;
and
 
 
  WHEREAS,   As  well  as  being  a  peace  activist,  Gloria  Steinem
continually worked on economic issues such as unequal pay for women, and
sought to bridge racial divides,  often  speaking  on  tour  with  Black
activists such as Dorothy Pitman Hughes and Florynce Kennedy; and
 
  WHEREAS,  Over  the  years,  Gloria Steinem helped found a number of
organizations, including the Women's Action Alliance, Voters for  Choice
(later the Planned Parenthood Action Fund), the Women's Media Center and
the  Ms. Foundation for Women; through the latter, Steinem played a role
in the birth of Take Our Daughters to Work Day, which was conceived as a
New York City event to increase the self-esteem of girls; and
 
  WHEREAS, Gloria Steinem's influence and work have  thrust  her  into
the  national  zeitgeist many times over her decades of activism and she
has been referenced and portrayed in a number  of  productions;  Kirstie
Alley  starred  as her in 1985, in "A Bunny's Tale," a TV movie based on
Steinem's Playboy Club experience and in 2020, there was "The  Glorias,"
a  movie  in  which  she  was played by different actresses in different
stages of her life, and "Mrs. America," which dramatized the battle over
the Equal Rights Amendment where Rose Byrne  portrayed  her,  with  Cate
Blanchett as Schlafly and Margo Martindale as Abzug; and
 
  WHEREAS, HBO produced a 2011 documentary about Gloria Steinem titled
"Gloria:  In  Her  Own  Words," and she was also prominently featured in
"Dear Ms.: A Revolution in Print," a 2025 HBO documentary; and
 
  WHEREAS, In the near century of her life, Gloria  Steinem  penned  a
multitude  of  articles  and books, including her 1983 book, "Outrageous
Acts and Everyday  Rebellions,"  dedicated  to  challenging  the  social
constructs of the world she was born into; and
 
  WHEREAS,  Citing  the  influence  she had on his social perspective,
President Barack Obama awarded Gloria Steinem the Presidential Medal  of
Freedom in 2013; and
 
  WHEREAS,  Armed  with a humanistic spirit and imbued with a sense of
compassion, Gloria Steinem leaves behind a legacy and  a  body  of  work
which  will  long  endure  the  passage  of  time  and  will continue to
influence and inspire  future  generations  to  question  and  challenge
inequity; now, therefore, be it
 
  RESOLVED,  That  this Legislative Body pause in its deliberations to
mourn the death of Gloria  Steinem,  social  journalist,  activist,  and
Feminist icon; and be it further
 
  RESOLVED,  That  a  copy  of this Resolution, suitably engrossed, be
transmitted to the family of Gloria Steinem.
