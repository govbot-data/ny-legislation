Add §131-cc, Soc Serv L
 
Creates a true cost of economic security study to measure how much income and other resources are needed for a family of a given composition in a given geographic location to adequately meet a comprehensive set of regular household costs, set aside savings both for future planning and for short-term emergencies, and manage debt.

        Introduced  by  Sen. PERSAUD -- read twice and ordered printed, and when
          printed to be committed to the Committee on Rules
 
        AN ACT to amend the social services law, in relation to the creation  of
          a true cost of economic security study
 
          The  People of the State of New York, represented in Senate and Assem-
        bly, do enact as follows:

     1    Section 1. The social services law is amended by adding a new  section
     2  131-cc to read as follows:
     3    §  131-cc. True cost of economic security study. 1. Study established.
     4  (a) The true cost of economic security (TCES) measures how  much  income
     5  and  other resources are needed for a family of a given composition in a
     6  given geographic location to adequately  meet  a  comprehensive  set  of
     7  regular  household costs, set aside savings both for future planning and
     8  for short-term emergencies, and manage debt.
     9    (b) The TCES is a tool that can be used to: (i) create a benchmark for
    10  measuring  the  effectiveness  of  proposals,  programs,  and   policies
    11  intended to increase economic security; (ii) identify where existing and
    12  proposed policies may be unintentionally increasing economic insecurity;
    13  (iii)  assist policymakers in identifying and addressing cost areas that
    14  consume a disproportionate share of New Yorkers' resources, contributing
    15  to economic insecurity; (iv) identify geographical areas of concentrated
    16  economic insecurity and target interventions; (v)  determine  the  wages
    17  and  other resources necessary for New Yorkers to achieve economic secu-
    18  rity; and (vi) enhance online  "budget  calculator"  tools  that  enable
    19  users  to  strategize  combinations  of  public and private supports and
    20  subsidies, with training and education, and/or employment,  to  increase
    21  income and economic security.
    22    2.  Collection  and  analysis.  The department of labor shall contract
    23  with a third party, that meets the requirements in subdivision  four  of
    24  this  section,  for  the collection and analysis of data that results in
    25  the calculation of the true cost of economic security.
    26    3. Calculation of the true cost of economic security measure. (a)  The
    27  true  cost  of  economic  security  measure  shall:  (i)  determine  the
    28  resources required to reach a base  level  of  economic  security;  (ii)

     1  account for family size, composition, and geography to the county level;
     2  (iii)  utilize,  to  the  extent such information is available, the most
     3  up-to-date costs  of  housing,  a  reasonable  food  budget,  regionally
     4  specific  transportation,  health  care,  including premiums and out-of-
     5  pocket expenses, center-based child care, technology,  taxes,  including
     6  local,  state and federal taxes, and service of certain types of debt as
     7  well as other miscellaneous costs required to fully  engage  in  today's
     8  economy  and  society;  (iv)  account for income from labor earnings and
     9  self-employment income, government social insurance programs like Social
    10  Security, Medicare, Medicaid, and SNAP, tax  credits,  including  local,
    11  state, and federal tax credits, as well as employer-provided health care
    12  contributions;  and  (v)  include  a  savings  target  no less than that
    13  included in the most recent national  true  cost  of  economic  security
    14  measure.
    15    (b)  In calculating the true cost of economic security, the contractor
    16  shall utilize, to the extent practicable, data that is county-level  and
    17  nationally  standardized, calculated annually, and from reliable govern-
    18  ment or academic/scholarly sources, such as  the  United  States  census
    19  bureau,  United  States  department of housing and urban development, or
    20  any other data reported to state and federal agencies using standardized
    21  methodology.  State or local data may be utilized  where  such  data  is
    22  more  targeted, granular, or comprehensive than available federal sourc-
    23  es. Data should be  disaggregated  demographically,  geographically,  by
    24  county  and,  where  available, by sub-county areas, for every region in
    25  the state.
    26    4. Third-party contractor. In selecting a third-party contractor,  the
    27  department  of  labor  may  consider the potential contractor's years of
    28  experience; the number of calculations performed for other  states;  the
    29  record of having produced such calculations and reports within the budg-
    30  et  and  time  constraints  of  previous contracts; the relevant skills,
    31  experience, and education of the  contractor's  personnel;  whether  the
    32  contractor is located in or affiliated with a college or university; and
    33  such  other factors as the department of labor deems relevant and appro-
    34  priate to evaluate the qualifications of such third-party contractor.
    35    5. Report. The department of labor shall submit a report of the  find-
    36  ings  required  in  subdivisions  two  and  three of this section to the
    37  governor, the speaker of the assembly and the temporary president of the
    38  senate, no later than one year after the effective date of this  section
    39  and biennially thereafter.
    40    §  2. This act shall take effect on the first of April next succeeding
    41  the date on which it shall have become a law.
